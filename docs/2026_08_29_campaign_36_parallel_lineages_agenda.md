# Campaign 36 agenda: parallel fixed and amorphous Ninereeds lineages

**Date:** 2026-08-29
**Status:** Proposed experiment agenda; not an execution authorization or frozen campaign contract
**Working track names:** Campaign 36A and Campaign 36B

## Decision to preserve

Campaign 36 should provisionally contain two independent learning tracks rather
than attaching an experimental cell substrate to the existing 1.2B model.

- **Campaign 36A — `ninereeds-1.2b`:** continue the existing fixed 1.2B
  lineage and evaluate the Campaign 36 curriculum now being developed under
  `training_data/v8_curriculum/`.
- **Campaign 36B — `ninereeds-amorphous`:** create a separate Ninereeds whose
  cognitive substrate consists of dynamically allocated cells. Train it first
  on the exact bootstrap program that produced the Campaign 36 starting
  checkpoint, then teach it with the same frozen Campaign 36 curriculum used
  for 36A.

The two models remain separate from inception. The amorphous model is not a
sidecar, auxiliary tissue, adapter, or descendant attached to the 1.2B core.
Neither lineage replaces, merges with, or discards the other.

The working labels 36A and 36B describe human-readable tracks. Mission Hub's
eventual machine identities and branch structure must be chosen through the
normal campaign-planning contract; this agenda does not silently introduce
lettered campaign IDs into schemas that currently require numeric IDs.

## Shared organs, separate cognition

Both lineages should use the same pinned sensory and expressive organ versions
where their interfaces permit it:

- one frozen LFM language ingress;
- one language projection into the native latent protocol;
- one frozen SigLIP2 visual receptor;
- one visual projector/resampler;
- one LFM expression path and intention interface.

The organs are not replicated per amorphous cell. Cells operate only on the
shared latent protocol after perception and before expression. Organ parameters
must be reported separately from cognitive-substrate parameters.

Across the two independent runs, the organ artifacts may be referenced from the
same immutable checkpoints. Each active runtime may load what it requires, but
duplicated runtime residency must not be misreported as independently learned
cognitive capacity.

## Source curriculum boundary

`training_data/v8_curriculum/` is currently work in progress and is not frozen
by this note. Its present contents include the ordered language lessons under
`language/` and the education and visual-material scope under `education/`.

Before either Campaign 36 track executes, the curriculum must be frozen into
one immutable, content-addressed event program. Both tracks must receive:

- the same accepted source bytes;
- the same lesson and event order;
- the same images and image preprocessing;
- the same corrections, delays, reviews, and retention probes;
- the same exposure ceilings;
- the same evaluation manifests and evaluation cadence.

Architecture-specific tensor plumbing and optimization may differ where they
must, but every difference must be declared. “Same methodology” means the same
teaching experiences, sequencing, evidence rules, and behavioral evaluation;
it does not mean pretending two different architectures have identical
optimization dynamics or equal cost per update.

## Phase 0 — Recover and freeze the bootstrap program

The amorphous lineage must first receive the exact program that created the
starting competence of the 1.2B Campaign 36 lineage: approximately 3,000 words
and 30,000 images, with the actual accepted counts taken from immutable
artifacts rather than this shorthand.

Execution is blocked unless the bootstrap course can be reconstructed with:

- exact curriculum and material hashes;
- exact accepted image identities and preprocessing;
- organ and projector revisions;
- event order, exposures, and stopping conditions;
- initialization and optimizer records where available;
- the behavioral and representation evaluations used at the time.

The existing 1.2B checkpoint remains the primary historical comparator. It is
not discarded merely because an exact fixed-architecture rerun may later offer
a tighter replication control.

## Campaign 36A — fixed 1.2B curriculum experiment

### Purpose

Measure the effectiveness of the new Campaign 36 curriculum on the preserved
1.2B architecture and checkpoint lineage.

### Primary observations

- trained-form acquisition;
- controlled generalization boundaries;
- learning rate by lesson, event, exposure, token, and wall-clock time;
- delayed retention and interference;
- retained prior capabilities;
- behavioral consistency and calibration;
- activation and MRI changes under the existing evaluation law;
- compute, memory, and optimizer telemetry without giving loss decision
  authority.

This track primarily studies the curriculum and the behavior of the established
architecture under that curriculum.

## Campaign 36B — amorphous growth experiment

### Purpose

Train an independent cell-based Ninereeds from its own minimal substrate and
observe how much cognitive tissue it allocates, uses, stabilizes, and later
makes dormant while experiencing the bootstrap course and Campaign 36
curriculum.

### Starting anatomy

The initial anatomy remains to be preregistered, but it should contain only:

- the shared organ interfaces;
- a stable latent workspace and expression readout;
- a small seed population of homogeneous cells;
- dormant capacity or a deterministic cell-allocation mechanism;
- bounded propagation and resource limits;
- complete lineage, activity, and topology instrumentation.

Cell birth should be enabled from the beginning of the bootstrap course so the
growth curve is observable. The birth rule, residual definition, saturation
test, initialization rule, random seed derivation, admission rule, and hard
resource ceiling must all be frozen before execution.

### Growth and shrinkage

An explosive early growth phase is a hypothesis, not an assumption. The model
may allocate rapidly while encountering the dense novelty of the bootstrap
course. Later teaching may produce any of several outcomes:

- continued growth;
- slowing growth;
- a stable plateau;
- specialization and reuse of existing cells;
- dormancy of cells made redundant by more efficient structure;
- reactivation of older cells during delayed or unusual probes.

The first experiment should define “shrinkage” as reduced active and admitted
substrate, with reversible dormancy before destructive deletion. Physical
reclamation must not erase rollback evidence or make retention failures
impossible to diagnose.

### Primary observations

- total allocated cell count and parameter count over experience;
- admitted, provisional, active, dormant, archived, and reclaimed cells;
- parameter births and dormancy transitions per lesson, event, and exposure;
- active cells, propagation steps, and active parameter-time per response;
- growth rate during bootstrap versus Campaign 36 teaching;
- settling time and peak cognitive load;
- recurrence and reuse of successful cell trajectories;
- coactivation topology and emerging neighborhoods;
- learning rate on the same behavioral thresholds used for 36A;
- delayed retention, interference, and compensatory recruitment;
- response to causal cell and neighborhood ablations;
- organ cost, substrate cost, transfer cost, and wall-clock cost reported
  separately;
- growth-policy failures, including runaway allocation, collapse, dead tissue,
  and self-reinforcing but behaviorally harmful neighborhoods.

## What the paired experiment can answer

The two tracks can be compared on shared behavioral evidence while retaining
their separate scientific purposes.

Candidate questions include:

1. Does either architecture acquire the same curriculum milestones with fewer
   teaching events or exposures?
2. How do their controlled generalization boundaries differ?
3. How do delayed retention and interference differ?
4. Does the amorphous substrate allocate rapidly during bootstrap and then
   plateau, reuse, or reduce active tissue during the Campaign 36 curriculum?
5. Does repeated competence become cheaper in active cell-time and settling
   time?
6. Does cell growth correspond to behavioral acquisition, or merely to novelty,
   loss, sequence length, or unstable routing?
7. Can dormant structure recover old competence after intervening teaching?
8. Which architecture fails gracefully under damage, perturbation, or limited
   active compute?
9. How much learned substrate is active for a familiar response compared with a
   novel or difficult response?
10. Do the two models develop materially different capability boundaries despite
    receiving the same teaching program?

## Interpretation boundary for parameter counts

The amorphous run does not reveal an architecture-independent true number of
parameters “contained in” 3,000 words and 30,000 images.

It reveals the amount of substrate allocated, admitted, retained, and activated
by one declared cell anatomy and one frozen growth policy while learning that
specific program. This is still a valuable measurement. Repeating the course
under different cell sizes, birth thresholds, propagation rules, or seeds can
show which parts of the growth demand are stable and which are artifacts of the
chosen mechanism.

For every checkpoint, report at least:

- organ parameters;
- total allocated substrate parameters;
- admitted substrate parameters;
- active parameters for each evaluated response;
- optimizer-state and persistent-state size;
- resident versus stored substrate;
- cumulative parameter births;
- dormant and reclaimed substrate;
- behavioral competence and retention at that anatomy.

The fixed 1.2B lineage provides a known-capacity behavioral comparator, not a
claim that all 1.2B parameters were produced or required by the bootstrap
course.

## Matching and comparison rules

The experiment should match what can honestly be matched:

- immutable teaching events and order;
- organ and preprocessing revisions;
- behavioral probes and scoring;
- evaluation timing by experience consumed;
- seeds and replication policy where meaningful;
- exposure ceilings and stopping criteria;
- checkpoint and evidence cadence.

It should report rather than erase unavoidable differences:

- parameters present at initialization;
- parameters created during training;
- optimizer and learning-rate behavior;
- FLOPs and memory per event;
- wall-clock duration;
- active versus total capacity;
- architecture-specific failure and recovery paths.

Equal experience, equal update count, equal active compute, equal total compute,
and equal wall-clock time answer different questions. The campaign must choose
its primary matching rule in advance and preserve the others as measurements.

## Required controls

- unchanged 1.2B starting checkpoint evaluation;
- untrained amorphous starting-substrate evaluation;
- fixed-cell amorphous control with birth disabled;
- deterministic replay of the same event stream;
- at least one growth-threshold or allocation-policy control if resources allow;
- random-allocation control where feasible, to distinguish useful growth from
  mere additional capacity;
- protected retained-capability and delayed-retention suites;
- complete failure and malformed-output accounting;
- contamination and overlap checks;
- architecture-specific resource ceilings and stop conditions.

## Stopping and invalidation conditions

Stop or invalidate the relevant run if:

- either track receives different curriculum bytes or event ordering without a
  declared comparison branch;
- the bootstrap program cannot be reconstructed to its declared evidence
  standard;
- cell birth is nondeterministic under the registered seed and event stream;
- the amorphous run exceeds its preregistered cell, parameter, memory, storage,
  or wall-clock ceiling;
- growth telemetry cannot distinguish provisional, admitted, active, and
  dormant tissue;
- evaluation changes after observing intermediate results;
- organ or projector versions drift between tracks;
- a pipeline failure is interpreted as an architectural or teaching result;
- aggregate loss is used to admit, reject, or declare learning without the
  required behavioral evidence.

## Agenda disposition

Preserve both lineages and design them together, but do not require either to
wait for the other after their shared inputs and evaluations are frozen.
Campaign 36A curriculum preparation can proceed independently of Campaign 36B
architecture implementation. Campaign 36B bootstrap replay can proceed without
modifying the 1.2B lineage.

Before authorization, a formal planning pass must turn this agenda into:

1. a frozen Campaign 36 teaching-program manifest;
2. a recovered bootstrap-program manifest;
3. a code-grounded amorphous cell and growth specification;
4. exact machine identities for the two tracks;
5. preregistered questions, controls, seeds, ceilings, and stopping rules;
6. a paired evaluation manifest;
7. an execution and storage budget;
8. explicit approval of any irreversible cell reclamation.
